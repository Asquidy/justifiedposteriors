This week, we’re joined by David Deming, top labor economist, blogger at the Forked Lightning Substack, and — since 2025 — Undergraduate Dean of Harvard College, to ask how the value of social skills will change in the age of thinking and talking machines.

We start with his QJE paper “The Growing Importance of Social Skills in the Labor Market,” which models social skills as a factor that reduces coordination costs, plus his new work arguing that meetings must be important because Norwegian workers spend about 12% of their hours in them. We ask how the coming Coasean singularity — the ability of AIs to communicate and bargain on our behalf — will change all this; David thinks that making routine communication cheap just frees up more time to argue about the questions that have no right answer.

We then turn to how this research informs his leadership at Harvard. Andrey asks about his research with Raj Chetty and John Friedman, which shows that an Ivy-Plus admission is a lottery ticket for joining the elites, and we talk about the cause of that finding, and how AI might change it. David argues that although social skills will be increasingly important, admissions shouldn’t select on them until we can measure them. We discuss possible new approaches to integrating AI into schools, including controlled first drafts, voluntary Yondr pouches, and oral exams. We close the discussion of Harvard with the faculty’s vote to cap A’s at 20% plus four.

The lightning round covers how he would allocate his social skill RPG stats, why ChatGPT use turns out to be “broad but shallow,” what economists get wrong because they lack social skills, and a tribute to David’s advisor Larry Katz. Most interesting in Seth’s opinion was David’s decision to favor composure and charisma over manipulation in RPG stats; in the bulk of the interview, he seems to suggest that raw charisma is less important than the ability to coordinate and smooth over differences in a work context, which would be closer to manipulation. Perhaps downplaying the importance of some social skills is the most important social skill of all…
