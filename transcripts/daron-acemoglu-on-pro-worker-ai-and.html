We’re honored to host Daron Acemoglu, Institute Professor at MIT and 2024 Nobel laureate. Daron has spent a decade arguing that AI should be built to make human expertise more valuable rather than to replace it, most recently in “Building Pro-Worker AI” with David Autor and Simon Johnson, and in a Nature essay on why we should stop talking about AGI. He also has a new book out, What Happened to Liberal Democracy?.

We came to steelman pro-worker AI and to find the tensions in it. The big question: is technology even directable, or is AGI the golden path to everything? Daron says technologists give him three reactions — “we’re already doing it,” “it’s impossible,” and “just wait for AGI” — and admits he can’t disprove any of them. From there: we discuss comparative advantage under superintelligence (Ricardo doesn’t save you if the other side is cheap enough), what he’d do if he were Dario (build a production line for applications, and move the knob from 95/5 toward 80/20 or 60/40), and why he doesn’t trust the market to sort this out.

We close with capital taxes and pensioners, whether Daron would join the AI-pause coalition (”slow down and pause are not the same thing”), what the US should learn from China on applications and classrooms, how he’d update The Simple Macroeconomics of AI for agentic AI. Lastly, we ask him what he really thinks of that Economist piece about him. “They’re the elite. I’m the working class.”
