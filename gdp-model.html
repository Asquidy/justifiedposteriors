Formal specification

The model and competitive equilibrium

The economy contains two production dates, two consumption goods, an investment good, N identical worker-households, and one entrepreneur who owns the capital-production technology. An equilibrium specifies investment, capital, production, consumption, and prices such that households and firms optimize and every market clears. In period 2, the entrepreneur’s factory stock produces successor factories and automated spacecraft without labor. The numerical illustration sets N = 100 and rescales period-1 GDP to 100.

  1. Period-1 capital-production sector

    Capital is produced rather than appearing automatically. The sector combines the entrepreneur’s inherited seed input B directly with labor LK. Its constant-returns technology is

    \[ K=NZ_K \left(\frac{B}{N}\right)^\eta \left(\frac{L_K}{\ell N}\right)^\ell, \qquad \eta+\ell=1. \]

    The calibration sets the seed and labor shares to η = ℓ = 0.5. The division by ℓ is a normalization that makes the displayed productivity parameter ZK easy to interpret; it does not alter the factor shares.

    Let qK be the period-1 price of installed capital and define gross fixed investment as I1 = qKK. Taking factor prices as given, a competitive capital producer solves

    \[ \max_{B',L_K} \left\{q_KF_K(B',L_K)-w_1L_K-r_BB'\right\}, \qquad B'=B \text{ in equilibrium}. \]

    Its factor demands satisfy

    \[ w_1L_K=\ell I_1, \qquad r_BB=\eta I_1. \]

    The entrepreneur reinvests the seed rent and retains ηK units of installed capital. Households use saving S to buy the remaining (1 − η)K units. Capital-market clearing is

    \[ S=(1-\eta)q_KK=(1-\eta)I_1, \qquad K_H=(1-\eta)K, \qquad K_R=\eta K. \]

    Period-1 labor is allocated between final output and capital production. With the period-1 wage and ordinary-good price normalized to one, feasibility requires

    \[ Y_{C,1}=L_{C,1}, \qquad L_{C,1}+L_K=N, \qquad C_1+p_{x,1}X_1=Y_{C,1}, \qquad p_{x,1}=1. \]

    Period-1 GDP is Y1 = C1 + px,1X1 + I1. The dashboard rescales this level to 100.

  2. Final-goods production

    Households own KH = (1 − η)K. In period 2, competitive firms rent this capital and combine it with the economy’s labor to produce ordinary consumption and conventional spacecraft:

    \[ \begin{aligned} C &= A_c K_c^\theta L_c^{1-\theta}, \\ X^C &= A_x K_x^\theta L_x^{1-\theta}. \end{aligned} \]

    The conventional sector satisfies Kc + Kx = KH and Lc + Lx = N. Normalize the ordinary-good price to one. Zero profit and common factor prices imply

    \[ p_x = \frac{A_c}{A_x}, \qquad Y_H=A_cK_H^\theta N^{1-\theta}. \]

    The numerical example fixes θ = 0.6, sets Ac = 1 + gc, and lets Ax vary. Factor exhaustion gives the wage and the rental price earned by household capital:

    \[ w_2N = (1-\theta)Y_H, \qquad r_KK_H = \theta Y_H. \]

  3. Household capital ownership and saving

    Each worker-household supplies one unit of labor and begins with one unit of period-1 income. Its preferences are Cobb–Douglas across dates and goods:

    \[ U_i = (1-\beta)\big[(1-\tau)\ln c_{i,1}+\tau\ln x_{i,1}\big] + \beta\big[(1-\tau)\ln c_{i,2}+\tau\ln x_{i,2}\big]. \]

    A household does not lend money to the entrepreneur. It spends s on installed capital, acquiring ki = s/qK units and receiving their period-2 rental income. Its budgets are

    \[ m_1+s=1, \qquad k_i=\frac{s}{q_K}, \qquad m_2=w_2+r_Kk_i=w_2+\rho s. \]

    Conditional demands are ct = (1 − τ)mt and xt = τmt/px,t. Thus τ is the share of each period’s consumption budget spent on space tourism. The intertemporal first-order condition gives saving supply, where the gross return is the capital rental divided by its purchase price:

    \[ \rho=\frac{r_K}{q_K}, \qquad s^*=\beta-(1-\beta)\frac{w_2}{\rho}. \]

  4. Equilibrium ownership and returns

    Aggregate household saving buys the household-owned share of capital: Ns* = (1 − η)I1. Combining this condition with the period-2 factor shares gives a closed-form saving rate:

    \[ s^*=\frac{\beta} {\displaystyle 1+(1-\beta) \frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}, \qquad I_1=\frac{Ns^*}{1-\eta}. \]

    Given investment, the factor-share condition determines capital-sector labor. The inherited seed input is set to the selected share b of period-1 GDP:

    \[ Y_1=N-Ns^*+I_1, \qquad B=bY_1, \qquad q_K=\frac{I_1}{K}. \]

    Households rent their capital to the conventional sector and receive all of that sector’s labor and capital income. The entrepreneur’s retained share, F2 = ηK, is the stock of labor-free self-replicating factories. Valuing each unit of factory capacity at Ac gives

    \[ M_N=Y_H, \qquad F_2=\eta K, \qquad m_R=A_cF_2, \qquad Y_2=Y_H+m_R. \]

    No exogenous interest rate is imposed. Once capital production fixes qK and final-goods production fixes rK, their ratio determines the return received by household capital owners.

  5. Goods-market equilibrium

    Let cR denote the entrepreneur’s consumption of the ordinary good and xR the automated spacecraft he keeps. He is satiated in ordinary consumption at \(\bar c_R\). Conditional on remaining factory capacity, φ is the share devoted to replication rather than current spacecraft:

    \[ U_R=(1-\alpha)\ln\!\big(\min\{c_R,\bar c_R\}\big) +\alpha\big[(1-\phi)\ln x_R+\phi\ln F_3\big]. \]

    The entrepreneur finances food by selling some automated spacecraft. Let \(\widetilde F_2=(1-c_R/m_R)F_2\) be the factory capacity left after those sales. The remaining factories split capacity between replication and spacecraft production:

    \[ F_3=R\phi\widetilde F_2, \qquad x_R=A_x(1-\phi)\widetilde F_2, \qquad R=2, \qquad L_F=L_X^A=0. \]

    Both activities use only the inherited factory stock. Competitive prices equate the value of one unit of factory capacity across the two uses:

    \[ p_xA_x=p_fR=A_c, \qquad p_x=\frac{A_c}{A_x}, \qquad p_f=\frac{A_c}{R}. \]

    Worker-households’ aggregate period-2 income is MN = YH, and MN + mR = Y2. Their space-tourism share remains τ. Equilibrium food consumption and the entrepreneur’s output values are

    \[ c_R^*=\min\{(1-\alpha)m_R,\bar c_R,\tau M_N\}, \qquad p_fF_3=\phi(m_R-c_R^*), \qquad p_xX_A=m_R-p_fF_3. \]

    The last term includes spacecraft retained by the entrepreneur and spacecraft sold to households to finance food. Adding conventional and automated output, period-2 GDP is

    \[ Y_2=C_2+p_x(X_2^C+X_2^A)+p_fF_3 =Y_H+A_cF_2. \]

    Factory replication and automated spacecraft production use no labor. The only labor used in period 2 is employed by the household-capital sector.

  6. GDP measurement and chain weighting

    Period-1 GDP includes household final consumption and the value of newly installed capital: C1 + px,1X1 + I1 = Y1, with I1 = qK,1K2 and px,1 = 1. Period-2 GDP is Y2 = C2 + px,2X2 + pf,2F3. Nominal GDP growth compares each period’s output at that period’s prices and therefore combines changes in prices and quantities. To compare investment across dates, treat installed capital and self-replicating factories as successive vintages of the investment aggregate and define

    \[ Q_L=\frac{C_2+p_{x,1}X_2+q_{K,1}F_3}{Y_1}, \qquad Q_P=\frac{Y_2}{C_1+p_{x,2}X_1+p_{f,2}K_2}. \]

    QL values period-2 production at period-1 prices. QP compares both periods using period-2 prices. The Fisher index takes their geometric mean:

    \[ Q_F=\sqrt{Q_LQ_P}, \qquad g_{\mathrm{real}}=Q_F-1. \]

    With more than two periods, adjacent Fisher growth factors are multiplied. Fisher real GDP is not additively decomposable, so the chart shows the transparent fixed-period-1-price bar while the headline statistic reports Fisher-chain growth.

Economic interpretation. Household saving is now a purchase of productive capital, not a loan of money. Capital requires labor to build. The entrepreneur is wealthy because his inherited seed input earns a share of the capital sector and he retains that share of installed capital; households own the remainder. The return on household saving is the equilibrium rental yield rK/qK. In period 2 the entrepreneur’s factory stock splits its capacity between successor factories and automated spacecraft. Neither activity uses labor, and both are included in period-2 GDP at their equilibrium prices.
